# Lanterns at Dusk

The county's answer arrived on a Friday, in an actual envelope with an actual stamp, which Harriet found almost funny. It said that there was no form for what she had described, that there was also no rule against it, and that the undersigned would consider the matter closed if the box were painted a color visible to drivers at night. The undersigned was the woman from the phone. She had added, in pen, below her signature: *Blue is fine. Mine is still in there, by the way. Second from the bottom.*

Harriet walked down to the corner at dusk. The new post was straight, because Walt had made it straight, and the box was blue, because Dev had painted it, badly and with great seriousness, over two evenings. The lanterns along the lane came on one by one as the light went, the way they had every evening for longer than anyone could remember, and for the first time Harriet understood that this was not a street with lanterns on it but a lane that had been named, carefully, by someone who wanted you to find your way.

She lifted the lid.

The letters were back -- dried on cooling racks, flattened under cookbooks, returned to their places in no particular order. The seed packet was there. The napkin with the dog. And second from the bottom, an envelope she had never let herself open.

She opened it now.

*To the new postmistress,* it said. *I am the person who used to empty this box, before I moved to the county office and started answering phones. I left when I stopped believing anyone would write back. I was wrong. If you're reading this, it means the box is still here, and so is the lane, and so, I hope, are you. Nobody takes this job on purpose. It takes you. Welcome to Alder Bend.*

***

Harriet stood with the letter in the last of the light. Up the lane, Second Rise glowed yellow; she could see Marigold inside showing Walt, with enormous patience, how to fold a crust, and Dev at the counter doing homework with a cinnamon roll for a paperweight. Rosalind was on the bench by the river with her thermos, pretending not to watch.

Harriet took a pencil from her coat pocket -- she had started carrying one, somewhere along the way -- and wrote on the back of the county's letter.

*For whoever is up at 3 a.m.,* she wrote. *I am too. The bakery light goes on at four. Come early and I'll introduce you.*

She put it on top, where it would be found first, and closed the lid, and walked home under the lanterns with her hands in her pockets and nothing at all on her clipboard.
