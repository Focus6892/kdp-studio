# Marigold and the Map

The bakery at the top of Lantern Lane was called Second Rise, and the woman who owned it, Marigold Teng, could not read a map to save her life. This was a known fact in Alder Bend, like the depth of the river at the bend or the exact hour Walt closed the hardware store, which was whenever the baseball game ended.

So when a folded map appeared in her flour bin one Thursday morning, Marigold's first feeling was not curiosity but dread.

It was hand-drawn in blue ballpoint on the back of a bakery bag -- her own bakery bag -- and showed the lane, the river, the bridge, and an X in the middle of the water meadow with the words *Bring a fork.*

"Someone is playing a joke," she told Rosalind, who came in every day at seven for a rye roll and the news.

"Someone is inviting you to something," said Rosalind. "Those are different."

"I can't read it."

"Then I'll read it to you," said Rosalind, "and you can bring the fork."

***

The X turned out to be a card table in the tall grass, set with two chairs, a checked cloth, and a plum tart under a glass dome. Behind it stood Walt, holding two forks and looking like a man who had rehearsed a speech and then set it down somewhere and could not find it.

"I made the tart," he said. "From your recipe. The one in the paper. It came out wrong, but I thought you should see it anyway."

Marigold sat down. She cut the tart, which was indeed wrong -- the crust was tough and the plums had slid to one side like passengers on a turning bus -- and she ate a whole slice without speaking.

"Why the map?" she asked finally.

Walt turned the fork over in his fingers. "Because you'd have said no to a question," he said. "And nobody says no to a map. They just get lost on the way."

Marigold laughed, which surprised them both, and then she drew him a better map on the tablecloth with a plum skin, one that led from the water meadow back to the bakery and ended in a little drawing of a door.

"Tuesday," she said. "Six o'clock. I'll teach you crust. Bring the fork."
