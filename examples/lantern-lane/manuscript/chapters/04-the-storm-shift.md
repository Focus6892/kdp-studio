# The Storm Shift

The storm came up the river the way storms always did in Alder Bend, which is to say that everyone said it would miss them until the moment it did not. By four o'clock the lanterns on Lantern Lane were swinging, by five the power was out, and by six the river had come up over the path and was investigating the blue mailbox with the curiosity of a large wet dog.

Harriet Pell discovered that she was running toward it before she had decided to.

She was not alone. Walt was already there with a wheelbarrow, Marigold with an armful of bakery bags, and a teenager Harriet did not know with a skateboard he was using, improbably, as a bailing scoop.

"It's going to float," Walt shouted over the rain. "The post is rotten, I've been meaning to --"

"Get the letters," Harriet said. It was the voice she used at the counter when a line had formed. People listened to it. She was slightly ashamed of how well it worked.

They emptied the box into the bakery bags, every envelope and napkin and seed packet, while the water rose to Harriet's ankles and then her shins. The teenager held an umbrella over the bags and let himself get soaked. When the box was empty, Walt and Harriet lifted it off its post together, and it was lighter than she expected, like a thing that had been mostly made of what was inside it.

***

They carried everything up the lane to Second Rise, where the ovens were still warm and the dark smelled of cinnamon. Marigold lit candles in jam jars. The teenager, whose name turned out to be Dev, laid the wet letters out on the cooling racks in rows, careful not to read them, which Harriet noticed and did not forget.

"We'll need a new post," said Walt, wringing out his cap.

"We'll need a new box," said Marigold. "That one's done."

"No," said Harriet, and everyone looked at her. The postmistress. The woman with the clipboard. "We'll dry it, and we'll paint it, and we'll put it back where it was. And then I'm going to write to the county and tell them it's an official community collection point under my supervision, and if they have a form for that, I'll fill it out, and if they don't, I'll make one."

Nobody said anything for a moment. Rain hammered the bakery window. Then Dev, from the cooling racks, said, "Can it stay blue?"

"It can stay blue," said Harriet.
